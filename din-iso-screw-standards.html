DIN & ISO Screw Standards

A cross reference between the German DIN standards still used across European drawings and their international ISO equivalents, plus what thread tolerance and property class actually mean on a purchase order.

Request a Quote → WhatsApp Us

Why two numbering systems exist

DIN standards were issued by the German standards institute and became the working language of European engineering drawings. ISO later published international equivalents, and many DIN standards were formally withdrawn in favour of them. In practice both survive: drawing offices carry decades of legacy documentation in DIN, while new specifications and export paperwork increasingly use ISO.

For most head profiles the two are dimensionally very close. They are not always identical, and head diameter or height can differ enough to matter in a counterbore or a tight assembly. Where the fit is critical, name the exact standard on your drawing and we will manufacture to it rather than substituting an equivalent.

Thread tolerance classes

ISO 965 defines how tightly the thread is held. The number is the tolerance grade and the letter is the position, which sets the allowance between mating parts.

ClassFitTypical use
4hClosePrecision assemblies with minimal play
6gMedium (standard)The default for general commercial fasteners, allows for plating
6hMedium, no allowanceUnplated parts where a tighter fit is wanted
8gFreeCoarse work, hot-dip galvanised parts, easy assembly

External threads use lower case letters, internal threads use upper case. Unless a drawing states otherwise, we produce to 6g, which leaves room for a plated finish without binding.

Property classes for steel screws

ISO 898-1 marks strength with a two-part number. The first part is one hundredth of the nominal tensile strength in megapascals. The second is ten times the ratio of yield strength to tensile strength. Multiply them to get the approximate yield.

ClassTensile strength (MPa)Yield (MPa, approx)Typical use
4.8400320General low-stress assembly
5.8500400Light structural and general engineering
8.8800640The common high-tensile grade for machinery
10.91000900Heavily loaded joints, automotive and structural
12.912001080Highest standard grade, socket cap screws

Stainless steel fasteners use a different system under ISO 3506, written as A2-70 or A4-80, where the letter and number give the grade and the strength.

How to write a complete specification

A fully defined part removes ambiguity and prevents re-quoting. Include:

  • Standard: for example DIN 7985 or ISO 7045.
  • Thread and length: M6 x 25, with pitch stated if fine.
  • Property class or material: 8.8 steel, or A2 stainless.
  • Thread tolerance: 6g unless otherwise required.
  • Drive: Philips, Pozidriv or Torx, with size if known. See the drive types guide.
  • Finish: zinc, nickel, black oxide or a zinc-flake coating. See the finishes guide.

Example: ISO 7045 M6 x 25, class 8.8, 6g, PZ2, zinc plated. That is unambiguous in any drawing office in the world.

FAQ

DIN & ISO Standards FAQ

What is the ISO equivalent of DIN 7985?
ISO 7045. Head geometry is very close but not always identical, so where a drawing names one specifically we manufacture to that standard rather than substituting.
Are DIN and ISO screw standards interchangeable?
Often, but not always. Head diameter or height can differ enough to matter in a counterbore or tight assembly. Where fit is critical, specify the exact standard.
What does property class 8.8 mean?
Under ISO 898-1 the first number is one hundredth of the tensile strength in MPa and the second is ten times the yield-to-tensile ratio. So 8.8 is about 800 MPa tensile with a yield near 640 MPa.
Which standards does C S Fasteners manufacture to?
The common DIN and ISO dimensional standards including DIN 84 / ISO 1207, DIN 85 / ISO 1580, DIN 963 / ISO 2009, DIN 965 / ISO 7046, DIN 7985 / ISO 7045 and DIN 912 / ISO 4762. Made-to-drawing parts are produced on our custom line.
